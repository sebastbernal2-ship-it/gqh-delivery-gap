## 1. The rule we build by

The rubric scores Economic Foundation and creativity first, then risk, then evidence.
Criterion 5 (Performance and Analytical Evidence) is capped at 4 if the code does not
run, does not match the note, or shows lookahead or out-of-sample tuning.

So the build order is inverted from the usual hackathon build:

1. Write the mechanism and the falsifier in words.
2. Write the measurement contract.
3. Build the smallest pipeline that produces the numbers the note will quote.
4. Add depth only where a scoring criterion is still empty.

No return-forecasting model anywhere. A model may only produce a typed fact, a
calibration, or an allocation. Every trade decision must trace to a mechanism sentence
in the note.

## 2. The mechanism (frozen wording, do not edit after Saturday 00:00)

> The AI-capex complex is priced by a small set of names whose disclosure dates are set
> by federal rule, whose hedge demand is structural, and whose fundamental input is a
> public daily index of executed GPU rental prices. Two objects are therefore mispriced:
> the option-implied move on rule-dated disclosure categories where the hedge demand is
> structural, and the joint dependence between the complex's legs, which is not directly
> traded. We trade both residuals.

Two sub-mechanisms, both from the brief's own list of edge sources.

**Structural constraint.** An 8-K is due within 4 business days for most items. The date
is written down in regulation, so the flow is dated, not forecast. Before that date,
holders who cannot tolerate drawdown buy protection. Dealers who sell it are left short
convexity. Retail buys lottery tickets on the narrative name. The chain therefore
overpays for the implied move in the categories where the hedge demand is structural, and
underpays where the news is an upside surprise that holders do not need to insure.

**Liquidity provision.** Perp funding is a contractual, dated cash flow, and forced
liquidations are flow that must execute at any price. Both are flow you can be paid to
take the other side of.

Who is on the other side, in one line per engine, because the rubric asks this directly:

- Engine 1: the protected long who must shed drawdown risk before a rule-dated disclosure,
  and the dealer left short gamma and convexity.
- Engine 2: the single-name hedger who cannot trade the basket, so the dependence between
  legs is priced by whoever is forced to warehouse it rather than by arbitrage.
- Engine 3: the leveraged narrative long who pays funding, and the liquidated account whose
  size must be filled at any price.

## 3. The spine: a marginal-constrained coupling

This is the single mathematical object that connects all three engines. It is what makes
the pile coherent instead of a bag of tools.

Every engine is a statement about the coupling between a marginal that is read off a
traded price and a second object that is not directly traded.

- Option smiles determine the risk-neutral marginal law of each name.
- The intertemporal and cross-sectional coupling between those marginals is decisive and
  is not directly traded.
- The minimal-information coupling that matches all prescribed marginals exactly is the
  Knothe-Rosenblatt / Bass martingale construction, computed by Martingale Sinkhorn.
  Source: arXiv 2609.39256, Stochastic Knothe-Rosenblatt, light-speed calibration of
  stochastic local volatility models. It matches marginals exactly, stays closest to a
  reference dynamic, recovers Dupire local vol as the grid refines, benchmarks Heston and
  Bergomi, and extends to multiple assets.

The anchor is risk neutrality. Marginals come from prices, so the edge can only live in
one of two places:

1. **The physical versus risk-neutral spread.** The variance risk premium. Engine 1.
2. **The coupling.** The dependence residual. Engine 2. The spot-perp funding term is the
   same object in Engine 3.

Never in a fitted forecast of returns.

## 4. Engine 1: event vol on rule-dated disclosure

**Instrument.** Options on AI-capex complex names. Five defined strategies are allowed by
the bonus specification: long call, long put, covered call, protective put or collar, and
cash-secured put. Pick one category, one strategy, one expiry bucket, and defend it.

**Measure.** Implied move from the pre-event session: (ATM call + ATM put) / spot. Realized
move to expiry. Ratio = realized / implied. The whole challenge is built on this benchmark.

**Signal.** Category-level realized/implied ratio, computed on in-sample data only, with
the category chosen for a stated mechanism reason, not for the best ratio.

**Trade.** Sell the implied move where the hedge demand is structural. Buy it where the
chain underprices an upside surprise. State which side you are on in the note.

**Mandatory controls, all four.**

1. Placebo cohort: non-AI 8-K categories, same window, same measurement.
2. Horizon decay: 1 day, 3 days, 5 days, to expiry.
3. Expiry buckets: 3 to 6 months minimum, then a wider split.
4. IV crush decomposition: separate the direction P&L from the volatility collapse, so the
   result is not silently a short-vol position in a calm year.

**Falsifiers, written before the run.** If the placebo cohort shows the same ratio the
category has no edge. If the effect does not decay monotonically across horizons the
mechanism is wrong. If the edge disappears when costs double, report that and stop.

**Artifacts.** `results/e1.json`, `notebooks/e1_event_vol.ipynb`.

## 5. Engine 2: the coupling residual

**Object.** Three to five names in the complex with liquid chains, plus the basket or
index that tracks them.

**Steps.**

1. Read each risk-neutral marginal from its option smile (single expiry, single date, so
   the marginal is a fixed vector of strikes and prices).
2. Build the multi-marginal martingale coupling with Martingale Sinkhorn. Report marginal
   matching error, which must be at numerical tolerance, not approximate.
3. Compute the coupling residual: the SKR-implied dependence against the dependence implied
   by traded basket options, and against realized dependence.
4. Trade the residual: relative value between the single-name legs and the basket.

**Falsifiers.** Marginal error above tolerance means the solver is wrong, not the market.
A residual that vanishes once realistic bid/ask on all legs is charged is not an edge.
A residual explained by a known factor loading is the "simple explanation" the brief warns
about, and must be checked against Ken French factors before publication.

**Artifacts.** `src/coupling/`, `results/e2.json`.

## 6. Engine 3: carry and liquidation flow, with real execution

**Venue.** Hyperliquid perpetuals. Full historical data is published: bulk S3 archives,
L2 book and L4 order-book snapshots, funding rates, node-level L1 schemas. REST is limited
to 1200 weighted requests per minute per IP, so bulk retrieval goes through S3.

**Legs.**

1. Funding carry: receive funding from the side that pays it, hedged where a hedge exists.
2. Liquidation provision: supply immediacy into forced-liquidation cascades.

**Execution model, owned here, shared by all engines.** Participation as a fraction of
average daily volume, the square-root impact law, spread crossing, and a capacity curve
that shows the AUM at which net Sharpe reaches zero. This is what fills the liquidity and
capital criterion, which the brief says most teams ignore.

**Falsifiers.** A carry leg whose P&L is explained by directional beta is not a carry leg.
An execution model that ignores queue position on the L4 book is not an execution model.

**Artifacts.** `src/exec/`, `results/e3.json`, `results/costs.json`.

## 7. Tool layer: where the abstract elements actually belong

Each piece of the wider pile gets exactly one home. Anything without a testable prediction
is cut, and the cut is stated in the note.

**Coupling solver.** SKR / Martingale Sinkhorn / entropic optimal transport. The spine of
Engine 2. Also used in Engines 1 and 3 as the language for the price-versus-physical gap.

**Multivariate distributions and local versus stochastic volatility.** The calibration
problem for Engine 2: Dupire local vol as the refined-grid limit, Heston and Bergomi as
reference dynamics, the SLV overlay as the calibration target. Real, testable, and it is
the reason to cite the paper.

**High-dimensional sampling.** Scenario generation for the risk and capacity sections.
Baseline is quasi-Monte Carlo (Sobol). This is the control that any quantum sampler is
compared against.

**Typed extractor (JEV or Laya, custom trained).** A single-forward-pass typed decision
head over 8-K text: event category as an enum, direction, magnitude bucket, whether a named
counterparty appears, counterparty type as an enum, and an agreement-term bucket. Output is
schema-validated, so a bad extraction produces a validation error, not a silent bad trade.
Every signal in Engine 1 must trace to a validated field. This is the "we train our own"
piece, and its only job is auditability. It never forecasts a return.
Validation: held-out filings, per-field accuracy and calibration, purged and embargoed by
filing date. If accuracy is weak, the note reports the weak number and Engine 1 falls back
to the vendor category tags.

**Spectral theory and harmonic analysis.** Two real uses, no more. Random-matrix cleaning
of the correlation matrix used by Engine 2 (Marchenko-Pastur edge, eigenvalue clipping).
And the spectral gap of the Markov generator as the mean-reversion timescale that sets the
rebalance horizon. Also available: characteristic-function pricing for the option legs.

**Quantum: three decidable experiments, no alpha claim.**

1. QAOA on the trade-scheduling QUBO: maximize expected return minus the turnover penalty
   with a cardinality constraint. Compare approximation ratio and wall time against exact
   solve on small instances, MILP, and a convex relaxation, across 8 to 24 qubits, on
   HiPerGator. The QUBO is grounded because turnover cost is real bps.
2. VQE or Gibbs-state preparation of the Sinkhorn fixed point. Same fixed point, two
   implementations. Report marginal error and time.
3. Quantum or tensor-network sampling of the joint scenario distribution versus Sobol QMC.
   Report effective sample size and tail accuracy.

The honest verdict goes in the note either way: this is where the tool wins, this is where
it loses at our scale. That is the framing the rubric rewards, and it is the truth.

**Compute cost (ORNN / OCPI).** The Ornn Compute Price Index is a daily settled benchmark
of executed GPU rental transactions in USD per GPU-hour, H100 near 2.83 on 2026-10-02. It
is the public, dated, numeric fundamental of the AI complex. Use it as the state variable
that makes the mechanism measurable instead of narrative.

**Kdb+/q with Tiger Data.** Justified only if the L4 tick history makes a columnar store
earn its place. Decide in workstream 0 by volume, not by preference.

## 8. Measurement contract (set once, then frozen)

Owned by Workstream 0, checked by the note assembler. Nobody edits this after Saturday
00:00.

- **Out-of-sample rule.** Most recent 20% of the history or the most recent 2 years,
  whichever is shorter, held untouched.
  - Engine 1 with history from 2022-01-03: 20% is about 11.4 months, which is shorter than
    2 years. Out-of-sample is therefore roughly 2025-10-20 to 2026-10-02, and the in-sample
    period is 2022-01-03 to 2025-10-19.
  - Engines 2 and 3: compute the same rule from their own start dates and state the dates.
- **Costs.** Stated in bps per instrument, justified per market, charged on every leg.
  Report the doubling test. Options costs include spread crossing on every leg.
- **Multiple testing.** Report the number of variants tried and correct with the Deflated
  Sharpe Ratio. The brief cites it by name.
- **Fitted pieces.** Any fitted parameter uses purged and embargoed cross-validation that
  respects time order.
- **Plateaus.** Report neighbouring parameter values. A single peak is a red flag.
- **Capacity.** Participation cap against ADV, square-root impact, and the AUM at which net
  Sharpe reaches zero.
- **Reporting.** In-sample and out-of-sample reported separately, every number net, with
  turnover, max drawdown, and an equity curve.

## 13. Cut list, and why

- **Langlands, affine Kac-Moody algebras, grand unified theory.** There is a real
  mathematical kinship through Weyl-group integration in optimal transport and integrable
  hierarchies in random-matrix models. There is no testable prediction inside 36 hours.
  Cut from the note entirely. Including it would dilute the Economic Foundation score.
- **Visual or satellite datacenter buildout tracking.** The keys exist. A labelled series
  does not exist yet. Cite it as mechanism evidence in section 1, not as a signal.
- **Forex AI race.** Defer. A fourth front weakens the other three.
- **Any quantum advantage claim.** Report scaling and honest verdicts instead.
- **Any model that outputs a return forecast.** Not needed, and it would cost the
  Economic Foundation score.
